# MSc/MRes Project Talks 2026

## Purpose

The MSc/MRes project presentation is an assessed component of the Research Project module.

- **Weighting:** 15% of the total Research Project module mark
- **Format:** In-person presentation
- **Purpose:** Communicate and discuss the research project, demonstrate understanding, and prepare for the final report
- **Session size:** Up to 8 student presentations per session
- **Allocation:** Session date, time, location, and related details will be provided on Moodle

---

## Presentation Format

- **Maximum presentation time:** 15 minutes
- **Question time:** 5 minutes
- **Timing rule:** The 15-minute limit will be enforced strictly
- **Penalty risk:** Marks may be lost if the presentation overruns
- **Recommended slide count:** Approximately 1 slide per minute
- **Suggested total:** Around 12–15 main slides, depending on speaking pace

---

## Required Presentation Style

### Template

- Use a **UCL presentation template**

### Audience

The audience should be treated as:

- Graduate-level scientists
- Not necessarily experts in the specific project field

Therefore:

- Explain specialist terminology
- Avoid assuming detailed prior knowledge
- Provide enough background for a non-specialist scientific audience

### Slide Design

Slides should:

- Clearly show the work completed by the student
- Use high-quality figures where possible
- Present information visually on most slides
- Avoid large blocks of text
- Use clear and illustrative layouts

### Speaking Style

During the presentation:

- Speak clearly
- Do not rush
- Do not simply read the slide text
- Avoid reading a prepared script word-for-word
- Practise repeatedly to ensure correct timing

---

## Recommended Presentation Structure

### 1. Introduction and Background

Explain:

- The wider context of the project
- Relevant previous work
- Why the project is important
- The research problem
- The research questions and/or hypotheses

### 2. Methods and Approach

Explain:

- What was done
- How it was done
- Why the selected methods were appropriate
- How the methods address the research questions or hypotheses

### 3. Results

Present:

- The main findings
- Important quantitative or qualitative results
- Relevant figures, tables, plots, or visualisations
- Results directly linked to the research questions

### 4. Discussion and Conclusions

Discuss:

- What the results mean
- Whether the research questions were answered
- Key interpretations
- Limitations
- Future work
- Final conclusions

## Marking Criteria

The Project Talk is assessed using seven equally weighted components:

1. Slide structure and organisation
2. Research context
3. Methodology
4. Results
5. Discussion and conclusions
6. Oral presentation
7. Response to questions

Each component contributes equally to the presentation mark.

## Practical Checklist

### Before Creating Slides

- [ ] Identify the central research question
- [ ] Decide the main message of the presentation
- [ ] Select only the most important methods and results
- [ ] Identify limitations and future work
- [ ] Download and use a UCL presentation template

### While Creating Slides

- [ ] Keep the presentation within approximately 12–15 main slides
- [ ] Use figures and visualisations wherever possible
- [ ] Avoid dense paragraphs
- [ ] Explain technical terms
- [ ] Make clear which work was completed by the student
- [ ] Connect methods directly to the research questions
- [ ] Connect results directly to the conclusions
- [ ] Include limitations and future work

### Before Submission

- [ ] Practise the full presentation repeatedly
- [ ] Confirm the presentation is no longer than 15 minutes
- [ ] Prepare for 5 minutes of questions
- [ ] Check figure readability
- [ ] Check spelling, labels, units, legends, and references
- [ ] Export the final version as `.pptx` or `.pdf`
- [ ] Upload to Moodle before 17:00 on 22 July 2026

### Before the Presentation

- [ ] Check the assigned date, time, and room on Moodle
- [ ] Arrive early
- [ ] Bring a backup copy of the presentation
- [ ] Prepare short answers for likely questions
- [ ] Be ready to explain assumptions, limitations, and methodological choices
