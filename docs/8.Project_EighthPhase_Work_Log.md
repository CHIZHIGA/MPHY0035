# Eighth Phase Work Log: Unified Movement-Supported RSSI Pipeline

## Aim

The eighth phase consolidates the earlier project stages into one auditable
five-minute workflow. The purpose is not to reproduce every historical script,
but to apply the same state hierarchy and evidence rules to every parseable
wearable-and-beacon collection.

The implemented entry point is:

```bash
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/matplotlib PYTHONPATH=src \
  /home/czg/miniforge3/envs/0249_env/bin/python \
  src/EighthPhase/run_pipeline.py --dataset all
```

Individual collections, participants, and sessions can be selected with
`--dataset`, `--participant`, and `--session`. The repository's base conda
environment does not contain pandas; the existing `0249_env` contains the
required pandas, scikit-learn, and matplotlib packages.

## Unified Method

### Five-Minute Canonical Timeline

All timestamps are converted to UTC and aligned to a fixed five-minute grid;
the participant-local timestamp is retained in the output. For each beacon,
the mean RSSI and sample count are calculated. The raw room is the room of the
beacon with the highest five-minute mean RSSI. Low-coverage and empty windows
remain explicitly missing rather than being interpolated.

Accelerometer magnitude standard deviation is the preferred movement feature.
Where accelerometer data are unavailable, cumulative step count is first
converted to positive five-minute increments, with counter resets prevented
from becoming negative movement. Equal cumulative-count observations no more
than 35 minutes apart now reconstruct their intervening five-minute windows as
confirmed zero-step plateaus. Increasing and longer gaps remain missing because
the movement timing is unknown.

### Data-Driven Movement States

The log-transformed movement feature is clustered into two, three, or four
ordered states. A candidate model is accepted only if its silhouette is at
least 0.25 and each cluster contains at least 5% and 20 valid windows. The
smallest K within 0.02 of the best silhouette is selected. Only the lowest
movement state is called low motion; its boundary is the midpoint between the
lowest two cluster centres in log space.

The selected number of movement states also determines the awake RSSI windows:

| Movement K | Windows from lowest to highest movement |
|---:|---|
| 2 | 30, 5 minutes |
| 3 | 30, 10, 5 minutes |
| 4 | 30, 15, 10, 5 minutes |

These are trailing, not centred, windows. They do not cross recording sessions,
RSSI gaps, sleep/away states, pressure-floor changes, or a higher-movement
boundary. An awake window with no current RSSI remains missing even if earlier
RSSI is available.

### Sleep and Sleep-Room Evidence

All-day low-motion periods are joined with a maximum 15-minute interruption.
Candidates must last at least 60 minutes and contain at least 60% low-motion
windows. Candidate durations are clustered into two or three states. K=2
requires at least four candidates and K=3 requires at least six, with at least
two candidates in every class. Silhouette >=0.50 is recorded as strong;
0.45--<0.50 is accepted only when every adjacent duration centre differs by at
least 1.5 times. The longest-duration accepted cluster is selected as main
sleep. If these conditions are not met, the output is `sleep_unresolved`; the
method does not force the longest daily episode to be sleep.

Within a selected main-sleep episode, observed RSSI is locked to its dominant
room only when that room represents at least 60% of observed windows. A missing
run is filled only when the preceding and following 30-minute contexts each
contain at least one observation and at least two thirds of both contexts
support the episode's dominant room. One-sided, conflicting, and whole-night
gaps remain missing.

Because zero steps indicate no walking rather than wrist stillness, a
step-derived sleep episode can lock rooms or fill gaps only when its supported
dominant room is Bedroom. A supported non-Bedroom episode remains a behavioural
sleep result but does not change spatial output.

Known Bedroom metadata are retained as metadata evidence. When Bedroom is not
identified, the same beacon must dominate main-sleep episodes on at least two
different nights with at least 60% pooled support before it is called an
inferred Bedroom. Non-Bedroom-dominant sleep episodes remain possible and are
not forced to Bedroom.

### Probable Away and Missingness

RSSI absence is interpreted only after sleep candidates have been derived.
Outside main sleep, continuous all-beacon gaps are eligible for away analysis
only while a wearable stream remains available. Log gap duration is split into
two clusters. At least six gaps and two gaps per class are required. Silhouette
>=0.50 is strong; 0.45--<0.50 is borderline and is accepted only when the
long-to-short centre ratio remains at least two. The long class is called
`Probable away`; it is not presented as confirmed ground truth.

The final schema keeps `occupancy_state`, `behaviour_state`, and room in separate
columns. Therefore probable away, sleep, unknown data, and room location do not
become one ambiguous categorical variable.

### Optional Pressure Constraint

Pressure is enabled only when both wearable and environmental beacon pressure
are available. Environmental beacon offsets are tested for stable multi-floor
groups; each group must contain at least two beacons, have at least 100 overlap
windows, have silhouette at least 0.75, and be separated by at least 0.30 hPa
and three within-group MADs. K=1 is the explicit null outcome when no candidate
passes. This larger separation is intentional because beacon mounting height
and fixed sensor offsets can form stable subgroups within a single floor.
Configured floor knowledge is allowed but is recorded as a manual override.

Only high-confidence wearable floor estimates (>=0.75) constrain RSSI to
same-floor beacons. A floor change also requires local movement support.
Low-confidence conflicts preserve raw RSSI. In the 80-hour collection, 472
windows met the pressure trust rule but only five strongest-beacon windows were
actually changed, illustrating the conservative behaviour of this branch.

### EF-001 and EF-002 Regression

EF-001 selected an ACC low-motion boundary of 0.0184 and EF-002 selected 0.0213.
These differ from the exploratory fixed 0.023 threshold because the unified
pipeline re-estimates the unsupervised movement model for each recording. The
cluster centres and alternative-K diagnostics are retained in each session's
`movement_clustering_audit.csv`.

EF-002 retained the approximately 710-minute episode with almost no RSSI as
unsupported: its two observed windows had only 50% dominant-room support. It
was not forced to Bedroom. Fifty-one five-minute sleep gaps met the two-sided
room-support rule and were filled.

### K-Specific Sleep Evidence

The 80-hour and DH PanoH datasets each produced five valid low-motion
candidates. Under the K-specific rule, both support a two-class 2/3 split with

## Reference-Label Agreement

Reference files are loaded only after the complete prediction timeline has
been generated. The metrics therefore cannot change the clustering, away,
Bedroom, or correction rules. Results are described as agreement with existing
reference annotations rather than ground-truth accuracy.

| Dataset | Raw coverage | Corrected coverage | Raw end-to-end agreement | Corrected end-to-end agreement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| DH Paris | 62.8% | 96.6% | 28.1% | 61.9% |
| DH PanoH | 62.7% | 99.0% | 56.3% | 89.6% |
| DH Strad | 69.9% | 93.4% | 57.9% | 82.0% |
| KM Mal | 66.3% | 90.9% | 57.1% | 85.8% |

The large coverage gains in the first three datasets are mainly associated
with the separate `Probable away` inference matching reference `Out` periods;
they must not be described as RSSI room smoothing alone. Conditional accuracy,
balanced accuracy, macro-F1, end-to-end accuracy, and confusion matrices are
all retained so that coverage changes cannot be hidden.

## Two-Person Results

Home_A002 and Home_X001 are aligned on a shared five-minute UTC grid after each
person has been processed independently. Raw and corrected same-room windows
are reported for all data and separately for awake, sleep, away, and unknown
states.

For Home_X001, raw same-room time was 33.25 hours and corrected same-room time
was 32.67 hours. The correction added 41 same-room windows and removed 48,
showing that the result is not a one-directional smoothing operation. Home_A002
gained one corrected same-room window after step plateau reconstruction; its
co-presence changed from 69.58 to 69.67 hours.

## Step-Fallback Revision

The original step loader observed only the windows containing exported counter
records. At night, the device frequently emitted the same cumulative count
after a 31-minute gap, leaving the intervening five-minute windows missing and
fragmenting every zero-step bout.

The revised loader reconstructs only bounded equal-count gaps of at most 35
minutes. This added 1,136 zero-step windows for AB002, 392 for DH Paris, 223 for
DH PanoH, and 214 for DH Strad. DH Paris then produced 13 candidates and five
main-sleep episodes. PanoH produced five candidates with a strong 2/3 K=2 split
and now yields three main-sleep episodes. Strad's four candidates form a 1/3
split and remain unresolved. AB002 produced an abnormal 4,865-minute plateau;
the singleton duration class was rejected and sleep remained unresolved.

An initial experiment locked four Paris sleep episodes to their dominant
Bathroom signal. The reference annotation was Bedroom, and sleep-window
agreement decreased from 35.9% to 15.3%. The final rule therefore permits
step-derived spatial correction only for a Bedroom-dominant episode. With the
behavioural sleep state retained but the Bathroom lock removed, Paris
end-to-end agreement became 61.9% and transitions 267→253. Strad end-to-end
agreement became 82.0% and transitions 136→122.

This revision was selected after inspecting the DH reference annotations.
Although labels remain isolated from every pipeline run, the updated DH values
are development-set agreement and must not be presented as independent
validation. The next labelled collection should be analysed only after this
step rule is frozen.

whether it was directly observed, pressure-constrained, episode-locked, or

## Verification

Twenty-one synthetic unit tests cover:

* ordered movement clustering and K selection;
* low-coverage RSSI and deterministic RSSI ties;
* step-counter reset handling;
* bounded equal-count plateau reconstruction;
* preservation of increasing and over-35-minute interval-censored step gaps;
* exclusion of step-derived non-Bedroom sleep from spatial locking;
* two-sided and one-sided sleep gaps;
* exclusion of sleep gaps from away inference;
* preservation of awake RSSI missingness;
* adaptive-window reset after an RSSI gap;
* highest-movement five-minute behaviour;
* pressure activation and no-op fallback;
* two-night Bedroom inference;
* missing-room co-presence semantics.

The full manifest was then executed successfully for all 13 eligible sessions.

## Current Limitations

* `Probable away` is a data-driven interpretation, not confirmation of absence.
* Pressure clusters can still reflect beacon calibration or mounting-height
  differences. The revised blind gate now rejects X001's 0.231--0.233 hPa
  candidate separation and selects K=1, while the 80-hour collection retains
  an automatic K=2 solution at 0.381 hPa. This is conservative evidence, not a
  universal physical guarantee of floor identity.
* Sleep K=2 requires at least four candidates and K=3 at least six, with two
  candidates per class. This resolves the 80-hour and PanoH recordings but
  correctly retains singleton cases such as KM PanH Nov28 as unresolved.
* Existing reference annotations vary in origin and independence; agreement
  values should not be presented as universal localisation accuracy.
* A dataset-specific manual Bedroom or floor override is permitted only when
  its source and rationale are retained in the manifest and audit.
