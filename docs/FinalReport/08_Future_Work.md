# 8. Future Work

The first priority is independent validation. The pipeline rules and thresholds should be frozen and applied to new labelled datasets from participants and homes not used during development. Reference evidence could combine short periods of direct observation, time-stamped diaries, and structured participant review of pre-generated timelines. These sources have different limitations, so agreement should be reported separately rather than combined into one assumed ground truth. Prospective validation should also test room, occupancy, sleep, away, floor, and co-presence outputs independently.

The localisation model could then be extended without losing its interpretable baseline. Probabilistic room estimates could replace single winner labels when the strongest and second-strongest RSSI signals are close. Confidence could incorporate beacon coverage, sample count, signal separation, recent consistency, and movement state. Multi-resolution analysis may preserve the five-minute behavioural timeline while using shorter windows around transitions and higher-resolution pressure and ACC data for stair events. Supervised or semi-supervised models should be considered only when sufficient independent labels are available and should remain compared with strongest-RSSI and movement-supported baselines.

Further work is also needed on the optional branches. Pressure-floor inference should be evaluated in additional multi-floor homes with different beacon heights and environmental conditions. Two-person co-presence should be compared with participant feedback or observation and extended to distinguish shared room occupancy from likely interaction. Sleep and probable-away states could be assessed against sleep diaries, wearable wear-time evidence, and known absence periods.

Finally, the behavioural outputs should be evaluated longitudinally. Repeated measures of room use, active-period transitions, floor movement, sleep location, time away, and co-presence may reveal changes in routine or independence that are not visible in total activity alone. Real floor plans and interactive timelines could support participant and clinical review, but any future clinical application would require privacy assessment, robust missing-data handling, interpretable uncertainty, and validation across a substantially larger and more diverse population.
